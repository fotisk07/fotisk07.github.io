Before AI, most people got into programming writing simple HTML and CSS, maybe even some JavaScript. 
They would create a simple website, a game even, and get hooked.  

This was never me. I started learning programming with a pseudolanguage, then C, then Python 
became my daily rotation.


I. Hate. Web dev. Frontend annoys me, and my knowledge of backend and web development in general is
extremely limited. Cue the usual conversation: "Can you make me a website?" "Euh, no, I don't know how."
"Oh, but I thought you knew programming." Extremely annoying. Still, I have to admit that knowing the basics
would be useful. I had always wanted a personal site myself, but every time I thought about building one,
the prospect of writing HTML was enough to put me off.

But even before agents, couldn't I have used the myriad of websites and portfolio frameworks that exist? Yes, sure.
But this brings me to the second thing I hate, bloated websites. I just cannot do with this trend where websites 
get more and more bloated for absolutely no reason at all. It makes everything slow, clunky and not useful.
I like this philosophy: https://motherfuckingwebsite.com/ and the analog for blogging https://herman.bearblog.dev/motherfucking-blog/. Of course it is exaggerated but overall they are not that far off the mark.


But good news! In the age of agents it is extremely easy and hassle-free to build a simple and functional
personal website. I used Astra (probably overkill) and first prompted it to come up with the stack telling it 
to keep it simple. It recommended (which I also felt was right) HTML/CSS and Jekyll for blogging. I gave it some
website examples and it got to work. In around 2 min I had a prototype that I could then fine-tune. I gave it 
my CV to make the timeline and told it to search the internet to fill up the logos.

Most of what followed were small adjustments: bigger logos, a warmer background, different fonts and a dark mode. Getting the BCG logo right somehow took several attempts. Then we pushed everything to GitHub and enabled Pages. GitHub CLI was quite nice because the agent could deploy on its own without unnecessary clicking on my part.
